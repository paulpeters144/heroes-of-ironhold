# Hero Stat Definitions

These are the core stat attributes shared by every hero. Numeric values live on `HeroStats`.

## Health Points (HP)
The hero's life total and the single resource that decides defeat. Every incoming hit — sword slash, arrow, or fireball — subtracts from HP after armor or resistances reduce it, and the hero dies (or is downed) when HP reaches zero. HP also gates how aggressively the player can play: a hero with high HP can trade blows with enemies, stand inside hazard zones, and survive burst combos, while a low-HP hero must rely on spacing, dodge rolls, and crowd control to avoid being hit at all. In an action RPG, HP defines the hero's effective "mistake budget" — how many hits they can absorb in a chaotic fight before going down. Rest, potions, and healing skills restore it during or between encounters.

## Magic Points (MP)
The fuel gauge for every active ability the hero can trigger. Skills, spells, dashes with special effects, and combo finishers all draw from MP, so it directly limits how often the hero can use their most powerful tools in a fight. A hero with high MP can cast or chain abilities freely; a hero with low MP must weave basic attacks between ability uses and ration their best moves for the right moment. MP also creates pacing tension: it regenerates slowly (or not at all) during combat, so spending it all on one enemy pack leaves the hero vulnerable until it recovers. For spell-focused heroes it is as important as HP — running out of MP means losing access to their entire damage and utility kit.

## Strength (STR)
The raw physical power stat and the backbone of every melee hero's damage output. It scales the damage of basic attacks and physical skills, so a high-STR hero hits noticeably harder with every swing, thrust, and projectile they throw. Beyond pure damage, STR governs the "force" behind attacks: it increases knockback distance, letting the hero shove enemies into walls or off ledges, and it extends stun duration, making it easier to lock down targets for follow-up hits. Crucially, STR also works defensively — it reduces the knockback the hero takes from enemy attacks, so a strong hero stands their ground where a weak one gets pushed around. In an action RPG, STR is what makes a hero feel like a bruiser rather than a skirmisher.

## Armor (ARM)
The primary defense against physical damage. When an enemy lands a melee strike or fires an arrow, armor soaks up a portion of that damage before it ever reaches HP, making it the hero's passive "toughness" stat. High armor turns glancing blows into chip damage and lets the hero survive sustained assaults from physical enemies like soldiers, beasts, and archers. It does nothing against magic — a fireball or lightning bolt bypasses armor entirely — so it pairs with Resistances to form the hero's full defensive profile. In an action RPG, armor is what separates the frontline tank from the glass cannon: stacking armor lets a hero stand in the thick of a mob and shrug off hits that would shred a less-protected character.

## Intelligence (INT)
The magical power stat and the counterpart to Strength for caster heroes. It scales the damage and effectiveness of spells and magical skills, so a high-INT hero's fireballs, healing spells, and buffs all hit harder or last longer. Intelligence also expands the hero's maximum MP pool, giving them more fuel to cast with — so INT heroes gain both damage *and* sustain as they level. Beyond raw damage, INT can improve spell side-effects: stronger slow effects, longer buff durations, or bigger area-of-effect radii, depending on the hero's kit. In an action RPG, INT is what makes a wizard or mage feel like a force of nature, capable of clearing rooms with a few well-placed spells rather than trading blows one enemy at a time.

## Resistances (RES)
The magical counterpart to Armor. When an enemy casts a spell, breathes fire, or triggers a curse, resistances reduce the damage and sometimes the severity of the effect before it reaches HP. High resistance turns elemental attacks and magical hazards into minor inconveniences, letting the hero walk through enemy mage barrages or stand in burning ground without melting. Like Armor, it is a passive, always-on defense — but against a completely different damage type, so a hero with high Armor but low Resistance can still be torn apart by a caster enemy. In an action RPG, Resistance is the stat that decides whether a magic-focused boss fight is a deadly threat or a manageable one, and it pairs with Armor to create a fully rounded defensive build.

## Evasion (EVA)
The hero's chance to completely avoid an incoming attack. Instead of reducing damage like Armor or Resistance, Evasion gives a percentage chance that an attack simply misses — the hero dodges, sidesteps, or deflects the blow and takes zero damage. It applies to both physical and magical attacks, making it a universal "get out of jail free" defense that works regardless of what the enemy throws at you. Because it is a dice roll, Evasion is less reliable than flat damage reduction — sometimes you dodge everything, sometimes you get hit — but at high values it can make a hero nearly untouchable, especially when combined with fast movement and dodge abilities. In an action RPG, Evasion rewards aggressive, high-risk play: the more attacks you dodge, the more damage you never have to heal or tank.

## Accuracy (ACC)
The hero's chance to actually land an attack. It is the offensive mirror of Evasion: while EVA determines whether enemies hit you, ACC determines whether your hits connect with them. A high-ACC hero rarely whiffs, so their attacks land consistently even against fast-moving or evasive enemies. A low-ACC hero will see attacks glance off, miss, or get dodged — effectively reducing their damage output even if their STR or INT is high. Accuracy becomes especially important against agile enemies or bosses with high Evasion, where every missed swing is a wasted opportunity. In an action RPG, ACC is what makes a hero feel reliable: you can count on your damage to land when you need it, rather than gambling on dice rolls.

## Critical Strike (CTR)
The hero's chance to deal massively increased damage on any given hit. When a critical strike triggers, the attack's damage is multiplied — turning a normal hit into a devastating burst that can chunk an enemy's health bar or finish off a wounded target. It applies to both physical attacks and spells, so any damage-dealing hero benefits from it. Because it is a percentage chance rather than a guaranteed effect, Critical Strike adds volatility to the hero's damage: most hits are normal, but every attack carries the potential to be a game-changing burst. In an action RPG, CTR is the "spike" stat that makes damage feel exciting and unpredictable — a lucky crit can turn a losing fight around, and stacking CTR turns a hero into a burst-damage threat that enemies cannot afford to ignore.
